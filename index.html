Coordi-π: Coordination-Aware Dual-Hand Action Representation for Bimanual Dexterous Manipulation

Demonstrations

Coordi-π coordinates the two arm–hand systems through explicit hand identity and shared control time.

Abstract

Vision-language-action (VLA) models have shown strong potential for general-purpose robotic manipulation, but extending them to bimanual dexterous manipulation introduces a high-dimensional and structured action space. Existing bimanual VLA policies commonly concatenate right- and left-side arm–hand actions at each control timestep, leaving cross-hand interactions implicit within a single action vector.

We introduce Coordi-π, a structured action representation for bimanual dexterous manipulation. Coordi-π represents the right- and left-side arm–hand actions as individual action tokens and organizes them into an interleaved sequence, explicitly exposing the two sides within the action sequence. To explicitly encode hand identity and temporal synchronization in this interleaved representation, a learnable hand-identity embedding distinguishes the two sides, while sequence–control-timestep factorized rotary positional encoding (RoPE) separates sequence position from control timestep, allowing actions at the same control timestep to share a temporal coordinate while retaining distinct sequence positions.

Across all five bimanual dexterous manipulation tasks in DexJoCo, Coordi-π achieves state-of-the-art performance under both rand-obj and rand-full, improving the average success rate over π0.5 from 31.9% to 51.3% and from 18.3% to 32.3%, respectively. Coordi-π also consistently outperforms π0.5 across four real-world bimanual dexterous manipulation tasks.

Motivation

Bimanual dexterous tasks require the two hands to take complementary roles and act at the right time. In Photograph, for example, one hand supports the camera while the other operates the shutter. Conventional VLA policies combine both arm–hand commands into a single action vector at each timestep, leaving this structure implicit.

Separating the tokens introduces a second question: which neighboring actions happen together? R0 → L0 and L0 → R1 are both adjacent in the sequence, but only the first pair shares a control timestep. Coordi-π explicitly encodes both hand identity and this distinction in time.

Method

Coordi-π interleaves right- and left-side action tokens. Hand-identity embeddings distinguish the two sides, and factorized rotary positional encoding gives paired actions a shared control timestep.

Interleaved action tokens

Built on π0.5, Coordi-π moves the two sides from the feature dimension into the token sequence: R0, L0, R1, L1, …. In our setup, each side has a 22-dimensional arm–hand action. A 30-step action chunk is represented by 60 side-specific tokens, which are paired back into 30 joint, 44-dimensional commands for execution.

z2h = aRt+h,   z2h+1 = aLt+h

The policy receives three 224 × 224 RGB views, a language instruction, and a 46-dimensional proprioceptive state. Interleaving changes the internal action representation; it does not increase the number of control timesteps in the predicted chunk.

Hand-identity embeddings

Right- and left-side tokens share the input and output projections. Two learnable embeddings, eR and eL, tell the action expert which side each token represents. The corresponding embedding is added after the input projection and shared across all timesteps for that side.

hi = fin(zi) + ehand(i)

Even-indexed tokens receive eR and odd-indexed tokens receive eL. Both embeddings are initialized to zero and learned during training. This provides a consistent side identity without requiring separate projection networks for the two hands.

Sequence–control-timestep factorized RoPE

Token i has sequence position si = i and control timestep τi = ⌊i / 2⌋. The attention query and key channels are split equally between these coordinates. Paired right- and left-side actions therefore retain distinct sequence positions while sharing a temporal coordinate. The flow-matching training objective remains the same as in π0.5.

R0 → L0 has offsets Δs = 1 and Δτ = 0, whereas L0 → R1 has Δs = 1 and Δτ = 1. Applying RoPE independently to the two halves of each query and key represents this difference explicitly. The factorization is used for attention between action tokens; attention involving context tokens retains the original positional encoding.

Training and action decoding

Training retains the flow-matching objective of π0.5. Noisy action chunks are arranged in interleaved order, augmented with hand-identity embeddings, and processed using the sequence and control-time coordinates. The control timestep describes position within an action chunk; it is independent of the continuous time used by flow matching.

At inference, the action expert generates side-specific tokens conditioned on images, language, and robot state. Tokens with the same control timestep are de-interleaved and concatenated into a joint command for the robot.

ât+h = [ âRt+h ; âLt+h ]

Experiments

On DexJoCo, Coordi-π improves mean success over π₀.₅ from 31.9% to 51.3% under object randomization and from 18.3% to 32.3% under full randomization. On four real-world tasks, it succeeds in 60 of 80 trials, compared with 49 of 80 for π₀.₅.

Experimental setup

Simulation covers all five DexJoCo bimanual tasks. Object randomization varies object placement and table height; full randomization additionally varies third-person camera poses, illumination, and tabletop textures.

Task-level results

The largest gains over π0.5 under object randomization occur on Hanoi (15.3% → 48.7%) and Unlock iPad (12.0% → 52.0%), which require complementary roles and ordered cross-hand interactions. Under full randomization, Coordi-π retains a 14.0 percentage-point advantage in mean success, although performance drops for both methods.

On the real robot, success improves on all four tasks. Photograph rises from 13/20 to 18/20 successful trials, and Assembly from 6/20 to 10/20. Both policies use the same 100 demonstrations per task and the same training budget.

BCS averages role coordination and temporal coordination on a 0–100 scale.

Coordination and task completion measure different aspects of execution. On Hanoi, the interleaved baseline has a higher BCS than Coordi-π (89.75 vs. 86.32), but lower success (37.3% vs. 48.7%). Following the intended role and timing structure does not guarantee sufficiently precise execution.

The Role Coordination Score (RCS) measures evidence that opposite hands perform complementary task roles. It is invariant to swapping left- and right-hand assignments. The Temporal Coordination Score (TCS) measures how often a dependent operation occurs while its prerequisite is satisfied. BCS is their equal-weighted average.

BCS = (RCS + TCS) / 2

Scores are computed from simulator contacts, object poses, and task states using fixed rules shared by all policies, on the same object-randomization rollouts used for success-rate evaluation. A missing required role contributes zero; a missing dependent operation receives a zero temporal term.

Real-world evaluation

The platform is a TianJi Marvin Pro equipped with two BrainCo Revo 2 dexterous hands, an external RGB camera, and wrist-mounted cameras. Demonstrations are collected with a PICO headset and MANUS data gloves.

The largest gains occur on Photograph and Assembly. These tasks couple stable support with shutter operation or precise insertion. Improvements are observed across all four tasks, but the evaluation remains limited to this platform and these trial conditions.

Remaining challenges

Precise contact and insertion remain difficult. Coordi-π reaches only 7.3% success on Assembly under object randomization and 2.0% under full randomization. The results indicate that a structured action representation does not resolve every perception or contact-control bottleneck. Simulation and real-world success rates also reflect different data and execution conditions and should not be compared directly.
